# Milestone 119 — X25519 (Curve25519 ECDH): the first step toward HTTPS

**Goal:** begin the road to **TLS/HTTPS** — the one big gap keeping the browser
on plain HTTP. Modern TLS (1.2 and 1.3) does its key exchange with **X25519**
(elliptic-curve Diffie-Hellman over Curve25519, RFC 7748), so that's the natural
first crypto primitive to build. This milestone is foundational: it's a complete,
test-vector-verified X25519, not yet wired into a TLS client — the first piece of
a larger effort.

## What it is

`x25519(q, scalar, point)` computes the elliptic-curve scalar multiplication at
the heart of ECDH: two parties each multiply their secret scalar by the other's
public point and arrive at the same shared secret. `x25519_base(pub, secret)`
multiplies by the curve's base point (u = 9) to turn a secret into a public key.

The field arithmetic is over `p = 2²⁵⁵ − 19`, with elements held as 16 limbs and
64-bit products — **integer-only**, since the kernel has no FPU
(`-mgeneral-regs-only`); no 128-bit types are needed. The implementation follows
the compact, well-audited public-domain **TweetNaCl** design (constant-time
Montgomery ladder, conditional swaps via `sel25519`). A signed-left-shift in the
carry step was written as a multiply to stay free of undefined behaviour.

## Verified — against the RFC 7748 test vectors

A host test checks the exact RFC 7748 vectors:

- **§5.2 vector 1:** `scalar·u` → `c3da5537…77a28552` ✓ (exact)
- **§5.2 vector 2:** → `95cbde94…7aac7957` ✓ (exact)
- **§6.1 Diffie-Hellman:** Alice and Bob derive public keys from their secrets,
  exchange them, and both compute the **same** shared secret
  `4a5d9d5b…1e161742` — matching the RFC ✓.

Clean under ASan + UBSan + signed-integer-overflow. Compiles into the kernel with
its exact flags (`-mgeneral-regs-only -O2`), confirming the integer-only math
uses general registers only.

## Where this leads

X25519 is the first of several pieces a minimal HTTPS client needs: an AEAD
cipher (AES-GCM or ChaCha20-Poly1305 — we already have AES-128 and SHA-256 as
building blocks), HMAC + the TLS key-derivation PRF/HKDF, X.509/ASN.1 certificate
parsing, and the handshake + record layer. Those remain ahead; this lays the
key-exchange cornerstone and is independently verified.
